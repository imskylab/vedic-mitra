# Vedic Mitra 0.10.0

_Released 2026-09-07_

0.9.0 was the release about being understood. This one is about being **honest about what the app
knows** — and about saying so on the screen where the claim is made, rather than in a comment nobody
reads.

Three things run through it. The app now **names the conventions it follows**, because a calculation
presented without its convention claims more certainty than it has. The **roadmap became visible in
the app**, so what is planned and what is missing are the same question. And the project wrote down
**what it is allowed to say at all**, before widening into domains where nothing can be verified.

## ✨ New features

### The hub is a map of the whole app, including what does not exist yet

The landing was twelve tiles for twelve built features. It is now two levels: **Today**, for the
handful of destinations opened daily, and **Explore**, with a tile for every domain on the roadmap —
built or not.

A domain that is not built says **Soon** under its label and, on tap, where it actually stands, in
its own words rather than a uniform "coming soon". Vastu is planned and open to anyone; Ayurveda is
deliberately limited to daily and seasonal routine; the Arts need audio and images the app cannot
carry yet.

This is not a teaser. It is the roadmap, and a test pins the tiles against the roadmap's own domain
ids so the two cannot drift apart.

### The month scheme is a choice, and the app says which one it is using

Under **purnimanta**, used across much of North India, a lunar month ends at the full moon rather
than the new one. The two schemes agree through the bright fortnight and disagree through the dark
one, where purnimanta already uses the *following* month's name.

The app computed one and said nothing. It now offers both, and **names the scheme on every reading** —
not only when it is the non-default one, because a reader whose almanac disagrees is exactly the
reader who would never think to look in Settings.

The engine is untouched: this is a relabelling, not a second calculation.

### A festival date says how its day was chosen

Which civil day a festival falls on, when its tithi spans two sunrises, is settled by **rule** rather
than by arithmetic — and traditions answer differently. The app judges at sunrise. It now says so,
wherever a festival date appears.

The three timed to a moment of night name that moment: **Krishna Janmashtami** to midnight, **Maha
Shivaratri** to nishita, **Diwali** to pradosh. Those are the ones an almanac is most likely to place
a day either side of ours.

### The day detail assembles a sankalpa frame

The ten measures that traditionally fix a moment — place, then year, ayana, season, month, fortnight,
tithi, weekday, nakshatra, yoga, karana — assembled in order and copyable in one tap. They are the
same values the panchanga already reports, which is much of why it reports them.

The year also appears in **three eras**: Vikrama, Shaka and Kali.

### Bundled hymns say where they came from — including when they do not

Every stotra and mantra now carries a source, and a new entry **cannot compile** without one. The
existing entries declare that their source is *not recorded*, and the reader is shown that rather
than it being quietly omitted. A test pins the count so the debt can shrink and never grow.

### Also new

- **The lunar month and the season read as wheels**, joining the nine limbs from 0.9.0, and the ayana
  and lunar year now say when they turn over.
- **The home strip shows whatever is next**, not only whatever is favourable: the window running now
  with *ends in*, else the next to open with *starts at*, either quality. An approaching Rahu Kalam
  was previously invisible until it began.

## 🔁 Changed

### Settings shows its choices instead of hiding them

Theme and month scheme were dropdowns, so the options existed only once you opened one. The month
scheme's whole point is the *difference* between amanta and purnimanta, which a closed dropdown
cannot show. Both are radio groups now, with proper single-choice semantics the dropdowns never had.

### Four of the most-seen labels changed spelling

The app displays several hundred Sanskrit-derived terms and had never said how they should be
spelled. It does now, and the audit found the vocabulary in better shape than that suggests — but
three words collided with themselves, twice a glance apart on the hub. **Today's Panchang** became
*Today's Panchanga*, **Muhurat** became *Muhurta*, and **Bal Sanskar** became *Child samskaras*.

The convention is plain romanization with no diacritics; the scholarly form is deferred to a
transliteration setting, which turns an irreversible editorial call into something a reader picks.

### The grid is called Explore, not Shastras

Most of these domains are not shastras. Four of the ten are Jyotisha or its output, one is a
darshana, three are Upavedas — the old label asserted a classification the app had not earned. The
roadmap now carries the tradition's actual taxonomy and says where each domain really sits.

## 🐛 Fixed

### A reminder was keyed on its own label

Reminder identity was built from the **display name**. Translating a label — or merely respelling one
— made the same window compute a different key, so the reminder was not renamed but **orphaned**: it
stopped being renewed and the Reminders screen could not reconcile it. To a user that reads as the
alarm feature quietly breaking.

Reminders are now keyed on an identity that no editorial decision can move, and keys written by
earlier builds are translated on read, so **an existing reminder survives the upgrade** — along with
its lead time and its ringing-alarm setting, which are stored separately under the same key.

This also fixed something wrong before translation entered into it: Saturday's two Dur Muhurtas are
displayed numbered, so a reminder set on any other weekday silently failed to match on a Saturday.

### Tapping a limb explains it — which 0.9.0 said it already did

The explanations shipped in 0.9.0, were tested, and were enforced by the build. No reader could reach
any of them: the rows took a tap handler that nothing passed. The 0.9.0 note has carried a correction
since; this release makes the claim true.

## 📋 Notes

- **Your reminders migrate.** Nothing needs doing. If a reminder set before this release does not
  come back after upgrading — or comes back as a notification when it was set to ring — that is a
  bug worth reporting, not expected behaviour.
- **The engine still has no Android dependency.** The panchanga explanations moved out of it this
  cycle so it can eventually be lifted to other platforms; its calculations are unchanged.
- **The app is English-only, and that is the largest thing it is missing.** Extraction of its text
  has started and is deliberately shaped as the easiest way to contribute — no Android or astronomy
  knowledge needed. See the roadmap.
- **Nothing was widened without a standard first.** `docs/knowledge-standards.md` sets out what the
  app may assert, what it must attribute, and what it will not say at all — no medical claims, no
  fatalism, no instruction in anyone's practice. It was written before the breadth, not after.
- Still no ads, no tracking, no accounts, and no network calls. Everything above is computed on your
  device.
