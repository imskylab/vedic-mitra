# Vedic Mitra 0.9.0

_Released 2026-08-31_

The release about being understood. 0.8.0 could compute a great deal about a moment and could only
show it as a table of names — which tells you what today is called, and nothing about what that
means or where it sits in a cycle.

This release adds two things that change that: **the app can now explain the panchanga's own ideas**,
in plain language, and **the calendar's day detail shows each repeating value as a wheel** — what it
was, what it is, what it becomes, and when each boundary falls.

It also fixes a bug worth reading about if you use ringing alarms.

## ✨ New features

### The day detail is a set of wheels

Nine values on the calendar's day card now show their neighbours: **vara, tithi, nakshatra, pada,
yoga, karana, the Moon's and Sun's rashis, and the moon phase.**

A bare "Tithi — Chaturdashi" says what today is called. Putting Trayodashi behind it and Purnima
ahead says it is a *sequence* — which is the thing someone new to a panchanga does not know and
cannot infer from a list of names. Each row also carries the two times that matter: when the previous
value gave way, and when the next one begins.

Past settles into grey, now is lit, next waits. A slim bar under each shows how far through the
current value you are — the only place karana visibly moves faster than vara — and when a value rolls
over, the row slides up to the next one.

Tapping any row explains what that limb actually is.

> **Correction, added later:** this was not true of 0.9.0. The explanations shipped, were tested and
> were enforced by the build, but nothing passed the rows a tap handler — so no reader could reach
> them. Fixed after release; see the CHANGELOG entry under Fixed.

Rows that are **not** cycles keep their table: sunrise, sunset, moonrise, moonset and the muhurta
windows are instants and spans rather than positions in a loop, and "the previous Rahu Kalam" is
yesterday's rather than a step back.

### The app can explain itself

Eleven ideas the app previously could not describe at all: the five limbs, plus paksha, pada, the
lunar month, the moon phase, what a panchanga *is*, and why the day begins at sunrise.

Tapping a limb used to produce "More details coming soon."

Each explanation has a short form shown **without tapping** — because clarity that only arrives on
tap is clarity most readers never get — and a longer one behind it. The tone is deliberate: what a
thing is, with real numbers, and traditional claims attributed rather than asserted.

> **Tithi** · *The lunar day — how far the Moon has pulled ahead of the Sun.*
> A tithi is the time the Moon takes to gain another 12° on the Sun, so a lunar month holds thirty of
> them. The Moon's speed varies, so a tithi runs anywhere from about 19 to 26 hours — which is why it
> starts and ends at a different clock time each day instead of at midnight.

### Dignity on the Spashta Graha table

Each graha now shows how well it sits in its sign — exalted, own, friend's, neutral, enemy's, or
debilitated. It is the oldest statement of planetary strength, and the thing every other placement is
read against: the same Mars means one thing in Makara and another in Karka.

Rahu and Ketu show a dash rather than a dignity. They own no sign, and the exaltations attributed to
them are not agreed between sources — so the app declines to pick one.

### Two more doshas

**Kala Sarpa** and **Ganda Moola**, on the Kundali's Yogas page beside Mangal. Both report their
working whether or not they stand: which grahas fell outside the arc, or which nakshatra the Moon is
actually in.

Kala Sarpa carries its named type — Ananta through Sheshanaga — read from Rahu's house.

### Also new

- A new logo and launcher icon.
- Today's Panchang, Festivals and Events are proper destinations now, so each shows its title once
  rather than twice.

## 🔁 Changed

### Kala Sarpa uses whole signs, and that is not the textbook rule

Almost every statement of the rule says the seven grahas must fall *between* Rahu and Ketu, which
reads as a comparison of longitudes. Checked against an independent implementation across 56 charts
containing 18 with the dosha, **the longitude rule was wrong on every single positive.** Whole-sign
containment agreed on all 56.

A graha in the same *sign* as a node counts as inside the arc even when its longitude has passed it.
On one sampled chart both the Moon and Saturn sat a degree or two past Ketu and the dosha still stood.

If you have seen this dosha reported elsewhere by longitude, the two will disagree — and this app
follows the reading that matched real data, which is also the one consistent with how it already
computes houses and aspects.

## 🐛 Fixed

### Alarms stopped ringing after a restart

**If you set a reminder to ring rather than notify, it has been silently downgrading to a plain
notification after every reboot — and after every app update.**

The re-arming path rebuilt each reminder without its alert style, so it fell back to a notification.
Nothing surfaced it: the reminder still fired, just quietly, which is the kind of thing you notice
weeks later as "my alarm stopped working" with no event to point at.

Worth re-checking your reminders after updating.

### A festival could hide that day's observance

Raksha Bandhan is Shravana Purnima, so it appeared under Festivals while the Events list skipped that
day's Purnima entirely and quietly offered the *following* month's instead. The same collision hit
Diwali (Amavasya), Maha Shivaratri, Ganesh Chaturthi and every Purnima festival.

### Dur Muhurta had no explanation on Saturdays

Saturday is the only weekday with two, and the numbered names did not match the glossary, so both
rows showed "More details coming soon."

## 📋 Notes

- **The circular Cosmic Clock never shipped.** It was built during this cycle and retired before
  release: it showed each limb at its own angle, so the five current values scattered around the face
  and could never be read together, and a ring cannot show what came before or comes next. The
  reasoning is kept in [ADR 0015](../adr/0015-cosmic-clock.md) because the findings were dearly
  bought. The wheels replaced it and do the job better.
- **The wheels cover cycles only.** Nine of the day card's twenty rows repeat on a loop; the rest are
  instants, spans, or slow context, and forcing a prev/next onto them would be worse than the table.
- **Dignity is sign-level.** Moolatrikona and temporal (tatkalika) friendship are deliberately
  absent — both need data this app does not yet have, and this change surfaces what was already being
  computed rather than adding new claims.
- **Ganda Moola ignores the pada.** Confirmed by sweeping the Moon through a full lunar month: the
  nakshatras that fire and those that do not never overlap.
- Still no ads, no tracking, no accounts, and no network calls. Everything above is computed on your
  device.
